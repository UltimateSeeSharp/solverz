### Part 1c. The statement, hypothesis by hypothesis (SURVIVES; never stronger than C22)
Read against C22.md (SylowTwo.lean 179-189):
- **Quotient.** `PPowers 2 (r*2) ds` makes Grp ds an abelian 2-group of exponent at most 2r, any such group up to
  isomorphism. `ht` gives |Grp ds| = 2^t, and φ is a surjection with c = |ker φ|. This is C22's "H a quotient of G
  that is a 2-group", restricted to exponent ≤ 2r.
  - `hcert` is CycloFacts(2, r), supplied only for r ∈ {1, 2, …, 128} via maPairs.
  - `[NeZero (r*2)]` excludes r = 0.
- **m and w.** `hm` is k − λ = m², with m ∈ ℤ and the sign irrelevant. `hw` and `hw1` pin w = v₂(m). They also force
  m ≠ 0 and n > 0, so there is no junk case: m = 0 would make `hw1` false.
- **w and t.** `hwt` is w < t, as in C22. A larger t only helps, so the Sylow quotient is C22's strongest form.
- **s and the conclusion.** `hs` is s² = k + λ(v − 1) with v = c·|Grp ds|, in ℤ (no ℕ truncation). `hkill` is
  2^(w+1) ∣ s, with any sign of s. The conclusion is the approved `¬ HasSDS G k λ`.
- **Proof route.** I checked the math by hand (it matches the proof in SylowTwo.lean 190-337):
  - Fourier inversion at 0 gives 2^t Y(0) = s′ + m·T + (sum over non-real pairs).
  - T is a sum of ±1 over the 2^R − 1 real characters, so T is odd.
  - Each non-real pair lies in π^(wφ+1), so the pair sum is ≡ 0 mod 2^(w+1).
  - Hence 2^(w+1) ∣ s′ forces 2^(w+1) ∣ m, which contradicts w = v₂(m).
  - Strength: w + 1 ≤ t is used only for 2^(w+1) ∣ 2^t Y(0). With w ≥ t the claim is false (C₂, D = (1, −1): (2, 2, −2), n = 4, w = 1 = t, s = 0), and
    the theorem correctly does not cover that case.
- **Strength vs C22.** The Lean form is never stronger than C22. On the table it is equal: the cap at 2r ≤ 256 never
  binds (Part 2a).
