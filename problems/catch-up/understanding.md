- Data (E34–E36, 2026-11-05): low blocks are parity-trivial on their whole column (C104: a ≤ n/2, every d ≤ n − 1, |m| ≤ 1, n ≤ 20; from n = 12 up to the block's maximum, and in height up to about n − 4 for odd n, 2n/3 for even n); tied odd blocks minus one number have |m| ≤ 1 at every height tested, low and rounds regime (C105, k ≤ 9), the first height-free family below 2p − 1; tied blocks minus one number never lose at the draw parity and draw at a = 1 (C106, n ≤ 19), which contains C1 ({1..N} = {1..N+1} ∖ {N+1}). - Data (E38, refuter): the one-hole band C110, odd blocks minus any one number at every deficit d ≤ k − 1 of the parity, k = 3..9, ending exactly at d = k; E38 ran heights 1..2(2k + 1) and (2k + 1)², and the refuter's engine every height up to k(2k + 1) + k + 1, past which C13 makes the value height-free, so every integer height for k ≤ 9. - Lean: C111, Catch-Up's margin lies between the two controller games where one player decides whether an exact catch-up ends the turn (`ctlMargin_sandwich`), so C1 follows if no controller wins at an even total (C112, `conjecture_of_controller_games`); data E39: true to N = 24, and the controller wins every odd total by exactly 1.
- Data (E41, scratch): the defender never needs long turns. In C1 (N ≤ 20) and C110's band (k ≤ 9, every height tested) the drawing player keeps the draw with turns of one number, or of the largest number below its deficit followed by one number (C114, C116; also C92's families to N = 16). The attacker's best turns do need three or more numbers at about a fifth of its turn starts, so the low phase is long turns on one side only (I27). It is a property of near-complete sets, not of draws: on low dense sets the short-turn defender loses 3 % of the one-hole draws and over a third of those with five or more holes (C116 Tried), and no simple rule picks its number.
- Data (I31, scratch): the draw at even totals is universal across catch-up-like turn rules: any stopping threshold within 1 of the catch-up point (and further ones from moderate N), turns capped at 2 or 3 numbers, asymmetric rules, turns whose numbers must increase or must decrease all draw at every even total N ≤ 15 (N ≤ 20 for some); only one number per turn (strict alternation) loses. So a proof need not use the exact turn rule (as C112 showed for the tie rule), and a threshold θ is Catch-Up with each new mover's deficit raised by 2θ, which the wide parity-trivial regions absorb (C110's width profile).
- Paper (unchecked): C104's no-loss half on odd blocks follows from C105 (exact change d, d ≥ a) and C86 (the minimum pick, d < a). Lean: C92's three draw families for every N give C1 (`conjecture_of_canonical_ray`); C104 contains two of them.
- In the rounds game the answerer's partner reply keeps the mover's slack e − c(R) from falling, for any perfect matching and for multisets. So a mover with debt ≥ c(R) is held to −(e − c), and only pre-switch states (debt < c(R)) are contested. There the pure row −max(0, d − c) can fail once pairs are broken ({0, 2, 2, 4}, debt 2: a mover win; C85 Tried).

**Blocks.** The low phase. While the smallest number is below the spread, a turn can take several small numbers to make change, and the value depends on the whole set: every local rule, bounded invariant and single strategy fails as N grows (C42–C46, C49, C54, C55). After any opening the opponent can make change with the bottom run plus one number and leave a block with holes at height up to about √(2N), where closed families, potentials and deformations all fail (C43, C58, C59). The lessons of both attacks: the draw belongs to the whole interval, and arguments that see less lose it, whether they move one hole (C64), use parity or pair structure (C65), keep only the minimum and parity (C66), exclude local reply cycles (C67), lean on small guard numbers (C70) or on summaries (C42). The draw quota is paid across many turns (C72) and cannot be reserved at the first pick (C68). Step-by-step descents stop after a few steps: in the tail length (C71), in height above 1 (C62, C69). On the pair-deleted family no single pick rule and no neighbor reply works: each fails in a few % of the sets, exactly on near-high sets (C81). Sharper bounds than C37 from the sum of the smaller numbers are false (κ, at C41's sets) or, like C37, not two-ply inductive: the change-making reply leaves a high remainder that no low-end bound sees (C37 Tried). Explicit certificates grow exponentially in N (C79). The band of blocks is height-invariant at integer heights only: interior entries flip on intervals between them (C88 refuted), so no descent through real heights; one-pick-per-turn play keeps the band only down to a ≈ (3p − 1)/2 (E28), so below that the band needs multi-pick turns of no simple shape (C86 Tried). The band is special to full blocks: it fails on collapsed blocks (E26), where only the single entry holds. One-pick play reaches lower on full blocks than any simple rule shows: no Hyb2 variant gets below 2p − 1 (E31), and on collapsed blocks no strategy keeps one pick per turn below about 4p (E32), though their real game draws at every c ≤ 2p; E24's rule lets the trailer fall 4p − 7 behind min R (E30), and neither a shape class nor C23's depth class captures where it is safe. What blocks both proofs is the same: the safe positions are not described by any local data tried (debt bounds, subset shapes, a maintained matching, C25's features), and no obvious fixed strategy draws the band (C85, C86 Tried); a proof needs an invariant that remembers more of the play, or a new idea. The new families do not escape this: the no-loss halves of C104–C106 and C86 chain to each other by one pick (exact change or the minimum), but their no-win halves, the leader's defense, need every mover turn answered, and no simple first pick works there (C105's no-win positions: the maximum fails in about 4 %, the others more). Whether to hand over a tie is no fixed rule either (ideator fresh07): a defender that ties whenever exact change exists, one that never ties, one that keeps the smallest lead, and one that maximizes the chance of an even split of the rest all lose C1 from N = 4 to 8 and nearly all of C110 (k ≤ 5), as do fixed choices inside C116's turn shapes; the attacker can force about N/3 non-level turns between level hand-overs. Natural Lipschitz-type inequalities break late (C117, C118 at sets with maximum 18, as C60 did), so C37 is the one universal bound of that kind still standing.

**So a Solution must.**
- Close the middle of C31 (b)'s ray: Q_N(b) no loss between the endpoints (C73), through C74's open hypotheses, C81 at j = 2 (interior), C82 (({3..N}, 1) draws) and C83 (boundary, perhaps only y ∈ {b+1, N}: C103), or through C92's three draw families, by an argument that uses the whole interval (C64–C67) and carries the quota across turns (C72).
- Or prove the band (C86) at integer heights, which gives C1 in Lean (`conjecture_of_band`): a rounds-structure strategy can carry it from C24's 2p − 1 down to about 3p/2 (E28), and below that the argument must handle multi-pick turns; no route through real heights (C88).
- Or prove the low column C104 or the tied one-hole families C105–C106, each of which contains C1; their no-loss halves reduce to each other and to C86, so the work is their no-win half.
- Or prove the one-hole band C110 at every integer height (Lean `conjecture_of_one_hole_band`); its high part is proved from linear height for d = 0 and for 1 ≤ d ≤ o + 1 − k with holes o ≥ k, and AM+T holds the rest of it there (C110 Proof), so the work is the low phase a ≲ 3k/2.
- Or prove that no controller wins the controller games on {1..N} (C112, Lean `conjecture_of_controller_games`); it holds to N = 24 with the controller taking every odd total by exactly 1, but the controller game is harsher than the real game off the root, so its defense is no easier.
- In every route the defender may be taken to play short turns (I27: one number, or the largest number below its deficit and one more), which are the rounds game's turn shapes; what the low phase adds is the attacker's long turns, which have no canonical shape.
- Be symbolic, a strategy or invariant for every N, not a certificate per N (C79).
- Other routes meet the same low phase: a value bound tight at intervals like C37 (C59), or a descent in height from the solved high regime past the integer heights (C55, C62, C69).
