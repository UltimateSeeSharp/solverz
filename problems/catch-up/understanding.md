# Understanding

**Holds.**
- Data: every even-total N ≤ 32 draws, and from N = 12 every opening draws (E1, E2), as other solvers and von Stengel (to N = 34) found (README); N = 33–35 come from one engine (E3).
- In our Lean: the parity law (C3), the level law and the two-token lemma (C28, C30), the score shift (C80), and catch-up-research's verified evaluator (Reference, Classifier, Executable); C1 needs only the leader's defense from two starts (C27, C29, C31).
- Lean in catch-up-research, not yet in our build: C31 (b) is a ray, V({2..N}, 1) ≠ win iff every Q_N(b) = (I_N ∖ {1, b}, b − 1) is no loss; its top five points b = N, …, N − 4 are no loss for large N (C73) and the rest reduces to interior and boundary cases (C74). Tools: exact-sum blocks (C77), increasing turns suffice for a defender (C78), every opening draws iff every pair is neutral (C75).
- High and small sets are solved: one pick per turn, the rounds game (C13); even blocks from height 2p − 1 (C24, Lean), pair sets above 3·max offset (C21); two pairs (C11), three pairs at deficit 1 (C76).
- Catch-Up's outcome is the sign of the margin game (C33); the gap bound C37 would imply C1 (data E17).
- Data: density protects, no loss with at most 3 holes (E21, N ≤ 25), near-intervals draw from m = 14 (C4); one-parameter families draw at every height (C32, C62).
- Data: C74's hypotheses hold for N = 11..24, and its interior case is the j = 2 instance of C81, intervals minus j ≤ N/4 adjacent pairs never losing at deficit 1, which holds to N = 28 (losses only with 6–7 numbers left).
- Data: intervals minus a few adjacent pairs are exact draws at every small deficit of the draw parity (j ≤ 3–4 pairs, d ≤ 4, N ≤ 28); the non-draws are mover wins whose deletions sit low under a long block (C81). C1 and all of C74's hypotheses lie in this draw region.
- Below height 1 the bottom twin pair peels by a mirror (C63, proved, refuter SURVIVES).
- Data: the generalizations of C1 hold wherever tested. Partially collapsed blocks draw at every height c ≤ 2p, p ≤ 10 (C85, E22); intervals with elements lowered by one draw to L = 14 exhaustively, random members at L = 23 (C84, E23).
- Data (ideator, unchecked): blocks draw on the whole band (every d ≤ p of the parity) at every height a ≤ 2p + 1, p ≤ 7, one exception (2, 2, 2) (C86); Lean has the band from a ≥ 2p − 1. The rest of the row moves with the height, toward the trailer. Aligned dense pair sets draw below min(k, p) (C87); any dense pair deletion never loses below |R|/2 (C89), N ≤ 18. Neither provable high-phase class (C20's partner class, C24's blocks) can be forced from C31 (b)'s starts (I19).
- In the rounds game the answerer's partner reply keeps the mover's slack e − c(R) from falling, for any perfect matching and for multisets. So a mover with debt ≥ c(R) is held to −(e − c), and only pre-switch states (debt < c(R)) are contested. There the pure row −max(0, d − c) can fail once pairs are broken ({0, 2, 2, 4}, debt 2: a mover win; C85 Tried).

**Blocks.** The low phase. While the smallest number is below the spread, a turn can take several small numbers to make change, and the value depends on the whole set: every local rule, bounded invariant and single strategy fails as N grows (C42–C46, C49, C54, C55). After any opening the opponent can make change with the bottom run plus one number and leave a block with holes at height up to about √(2N), where closed families, potentials and deformations all fail (C43, C58, C59). The lessons of both attacks: the draw belongs to the whole interval, and arguments that see less lose it, whether they move one hole (C64), use parity or pair structure (C65), keep only the minimum and parity (C66), exclude local reply cycles (C67), lean on small guard numbers (C70) or on summaries (C42). The draw quota is paid across many turns (C72) and cannot be reserved at the first pick (C68). Step-by-step descents stop after a few steps: in the tail length (C71), in height above 1 (C62, C69). On the pair-deleted family no single pick rule and no neighbor reply works: each fails in a few % of the sets, exactly on near-high sets (C81). Sharper bounds than C37 from the sum of the smaller numbers are false (κ, at C41's sets) or, like C37, not two-ply inductive: the change-making reply leaves a high remainder that no low-end bound sees (C37 Tried). Explicit certificates grow exponentially in N (C79).

**So a solution must.**
- Close the middle of C31 (b)'s ray: Q_N(b) no loss between the endpoints (C73), through C74's open hypotheses, C81 at j = 2 (interior), C82 (({3..N}, 1) draws) and C83 (boundary), by an argument that uses the whole interval (C64–C67) and carries the quota across turns (C72).
- Or prove the band (C86) at every height: from C24's a ≥ 2p − 1 down to a = 1, with only the diagonal d = p allowed to move (C88). Below the height where play keeps one pick per turn (E28 measures it) the argument must handle multi-pick turns.
- Be symbolic, a strategy or invariant for every N, not a certificate per N (C79).
- Other routes meet the same low phase: a value bound tight at intervals like C37 (C59), or a descent in height from the solved high regime past the integer heights (C55, C62, C69).
