# I27 Short defender turns

The defender of C1 and of C110's band never needs a turn of more than two numbers (C114): a number below its deficit as change, then one number that ends the turn. So the low phase is not "turns of no simple shape" on the defender's side; only the attacker's turns are long. These are exactly the turn shapes of the rounds game (C13, C22): a number that catches up, or an answer that stays behind followed by the next round's first number. So the defender plays a rounds-style game at every height, and the low phase is the attacker's long turns alone; the natural frame is a one-sided transfer (C22 with the attacker's turns unrestricted), with classes closed under any attacker turn. The change number can be fixed as the largest number below the deficit (C116, scratch), so each defender turn is one free choice. Source: us, 2026-11-05.

Tried (scratch `shape.rs` PROPS, 2026-11-05): no simple choice of the defender's free number survives; at N = 12, of 14,105 defender draw nodes, a drawing turn leaving the attacker at most 1 behind exists at 8,965, the least possible deficit at 8,508, one number at 11,286, the maximum at 5,535. Nor does the attacker's side take a canonical shape (`ashape.rs`): against C116's defender, turns made of the smallest numbers left and then one number are worse for the attacker at 2,003 of 9,422 turn starts at N = 11 and 5,180 of 21,321 at N = 12, so a domination lemma for its long turns would have to keep varied ones. Restricted to the defender's own tree (attacker nodes reachable while the defender plays a fixed drawing short turn, `reach.rs`) the canonical attacker shape is still worse for the attacker at 9 of 593 nodes (N = 11) and 18 of 1,253 (N = 12), e.g. ({6, 7, 8}, 11): rare, but no domination lemma. Adding every two-number turn to the canonical family leaves 1 exception at N = 11 and 2 at N = 12, but 53 of 10,333 at N = 15 and 136 of 20,281 at N = 16 (e.g. ({2, 3, 4, 10, 14}, 9), worse by 4): the exceptions grow with N. And the change number is not a reply to the attacker's own change (`react.rs`): allowed only after an attacker turn of two or more numbers, the defender loses C1 from N = 4 and 45 of C110's 140 positions at k = 3. Nor is it tempo control (`tempo.rs`): a defender that answers an attacker turn of odd length with one number and of even length with two, keeping the count of numbers left even so that it takes the last one, loses C1 from N = 4 (by 2, by 4 from N = 12).

Against a stronger attacker (it may end its turn anywhere from one behind to one ahead, `variant/extreme.rs`) C116's shaped defender loses {1..15} by 2 when the attacker opens, though a free defender holds; and in plain Catch-Up its good turn is unique at only 18–75 % of its draw nodes (N ≤ 15, falling with N), so the shaped defense is not forced enough to describe.

Along optimal play the attacker needs few long turns too (`short/turnlen.rs`, C104's low blocks, the defender answering optimally, every reachable attacker node): its best value is reached by a turn of one number at about 95 % of nodes, of two at the rest, of three only at a handful of nodes with a ≤ 2 (n = 12: 1 node; n = 14: 8 at a = 1, 3 at a = 2; n = 16, a ≤ 3: none). The 20 % of turn starts needing long turns (C114 Tried) were memo nodes against a shaped defender, mostly unreached. So along optimal play both sides play near-rounds turns; the attacker's long turns matter as alternatives the defender must answer, not as its best play.
