# I27 Short defender turns

The defender of C1 and of C110's band never needs a turn of more than two numbers (C114): a number below its deficit as change, then one number that ends the turn. So the low phase is not "turns of no simple shape" on the defender's side; only the attacker's turns are long. A proof can look for a defender strategy among one- and two-number answers, a rounds game with change (C13, C22's transfer has one-number turns), and must handle the attacker's long turns, e.g. by a domination lemma for the attacker or by classes closed under any attacker turn. The change number can be fixed as the largest number below the deficit (C116, scratch), so each defender turn is one free choice. Source: us, 2026-11-05.

Tried (scratch `shape.rs` PROPS, 2026-11-05): no simple choice of the defender's free number survives; at N = 12, of 14,105 defender draw nodes, a drawing turn leaving the attacker at most 1 behind exists at 8,965, the least possible deficit at 8,508, one number at 11,286, the maximum at 5,535. Nor does the attacker's side take a canonical shape (`ashape.rs`): against C116's defender, turns made of the smallest numbers left and then one number are worse for the attacker at 2,003 of 9,422 turn starts at N = 11 and 5,180 of 21,321 at N = 12, so a domination lemma for its long turns would have to keep varied ones. And the change number is not a reply to the attacker's own change (`react.rs`): allowed only after an attacker turn of two or more numbers, the defender loses C1 from N = 4 and 45 of C110's 140 positions at k = 3.

**Kill test.** C114 on more data: C110 at k = 8, 9 (heights ≤ 2(2k + 1)) and {1..N} at N = 23, 24 with the strict two-number budget. A lost draw kills C114 as stated (not C1).
