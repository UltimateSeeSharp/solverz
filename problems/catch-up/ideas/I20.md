# I20 Dense intervals

Prove that dense sets never lose (C99: N ≥ 9, I_N minus ≤ 3 holes, any d ≤ N of the draw parity) and get C1 from it (C100: via C31 (b), every Q_N(b) and every child of Q_N(2) is such a set). Their plan for C99 was induction on N by adding the top number: the even-top step reduces to the small-gap four-hole non-win at N + 1 and on to a shifted-tail package (C100 Evidence); odd tops have no step. A direct proof would start from the verified loss frontier (C99 Partial Lean: a loss needs an upper descent or a lower obstruction) and must close both branches under play, where holes grow (their stopping point, and C83 Tried's). Root claims: C99, C100. Source: catch-up-research (CU-L003, CU-L014; August 2026 final report; audit item X1).

**Kill test.** Cheapest first. (1) The two deficit-1 endpoint families of `even_dense_loss_forces_upper_or_odd_prefix_endpoint`, ({2..N} ∖ {d−1, d, h}, 1) with h odd, h ≠ 1, d, and ({3..N} ∖ {d−2, d−1, d}, 1) with d odd, 7 ≤ d ≤ N, every even-total N ≤ 28 (one outcome table, as `pairdel2.rs`): no loss → a loss in C99 at d ≥ 7 needs the upper witness (a block of ≤ 2 numbers and a winning opening w > d), the one case to attack; a loss → that branch needs another argument (no C99 counterexample). (2) E21 at N = 27, 28: a loss refutes C99 and kills the idea. (3) The small-gap four-hole non-win at M = 26, 28: a mover win kills the even step as stated.
