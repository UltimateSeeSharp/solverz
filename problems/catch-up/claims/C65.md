# C65 Pair deletions forbid neighbor jumps

**Statement.** If R is {2..N} minus disjoint adjacent pairs (so its sorted numbers alternate in parity) and y−1, y, y+1 ∈ R, then V(R ∖ {y−1, y}, 1) = loss and V(R ∖ {y, y+1}, 1) = win do not both hold.
**Evidence.** Refuted. catch-up-research@b0853b4, `CatchUp/BoCowgill/AdjacentHoleExchangeObstruction.lean` (CU-L023): R* = {4,5,6,11,14,15,18,23} is {2..23} minus seven disjoint pairs (`parity_root_pair_erasure`); at y = 5, V({6,11,14,15,18,23}, 1) = loss and V({4,11,14,15,18,23}, 1) = win (`alternating_parity_jump`). A jump does not force a winning opening: after opening 5 of R* the reply 11 draws (`parity_root_opening_five_draw`, `parity_root_reply_eleven_draw`). Recheck: `position_value 1 6 11 14 15 18 23` → L (every pick L); `position_value 1 4 11 14 15 18 23` → W (11:W); `set_value 4 5 6 11 14 15 18 23` → D (opening 5:D).
Rules out: deriving no-jump from parity or pair-deletion structure; the one-pair family of C64 is still open.
