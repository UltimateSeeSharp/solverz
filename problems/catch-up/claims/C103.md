# C103 C74's boundary shrinks

**Statement.** R_b = {2..N} ∖ {b−1, b}. (a) For 3 ≤ b ≤ N: if no opening w ≥ b + 1 of the tied R_b wins (V(R_b ∖ {w}, w) ≠ loss for every w ∈ R_b with w ≥ b + 1), then Q_N(b) ≠ loss. (b) For N ≥ 2: if ({3..N}, 1) draws and for every 3 ≤ b ≤ N: (interior) V(R_b ∖ {y−1, y}, 1) ≠ loss whenever y ≥ b + 2 and y + 1 ≤ N; (boundary) V(R_b ∖ {y}, y) ≠ loss for y ∈ R_b ∩ {b + 1, N}; then V(I_N) = draw.

**Use.** C74 with fewer hypotheses: its boundary cases y = 2 and y = b − 2 (so C83 shrinks to y ∈ {b + 1, N}) and its interior cases y ≤ b − 2 are not needed; C81 at j = 2 is needed only with the second pair above b. C74's proof asks that the tied R_b is no win at all, more than Q_N(b) ≠ loss needs. With C102 (a) the interior case can be no-jump instead. At b = 6, C101's `interval_seven_shadow_nonloss` handles y = 7 = b + 1 under a parent loss. Our inference (audit B's last note, 2026-11-05), not in catch-up-research; flagged for the refuter.
**Proof.** Sketch (paper, from verified lemmas). (a) C77 (b) (`Position.value_ne_loss_of_exact_sum_residuals`, `CatchUp/ExactSumResiduals.lean`) with S = I_N ∖ {1, b} (Q_N(b)'s set), A = {b − 1}, d = c = b − 1. Its hypotheses hold exactly: c ∈ A; b − 1 ≥ 2 is positive; b − 1 ∈ S since 2 ≤ b − 1 ≤ N − 1 and b − 1 ≠ b; sum(A) = d. Then S ∖ A = R_b, and its condition asks that no w ∈ R_b with w > b − 1 wins as an opening; as b ∉ R_b, that is w ≥ b + 1. No parity, no bound on N. (b) Q_N(2) = ({3..N}, 1) draws. For b ≥ 3 let w ∈ R_b, w ≥ b + 1, win as an opening. If w − 1, w + 1 ∈ R_b, then w ≥ b + 2 (w − 1 ≠ b), w + 1 ≤ N, and `after_opening_loss_forces_neighbor_value_jump` (`CatchUp/Earlier/DeficitWinningBound.lean`) gives V(R_b ∖ {w−1, w}, 1) = loss, against the interior hypothesis. Otherwise w − 1 = b (w − 1 = b − 1 is impossible) or w + 1 > N (w + 1 ∈ {b−1, b} is impossible), so w ∈ {b + 1, N}, against the boundary hypothesis. So every Q_N(b) ≠ loss by (a), and `value_Icc_eq_draw_of_lower_pair_deletion_nonloss` (N ≥ 2, C31 Evidence) gives V(I_N) = draw.
