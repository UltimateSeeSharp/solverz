# C85 Partially collapsed blocks draw (PBT)

For all c ≥ 1, p ≥ 1 and t ∈ {0, 1}^p: V(⊎_{i<p} {c + 2i, c + 2i + t_i}, |t| mod 2) = draw.

**Use.** t = 1…1 is C12 (TI): C1 at c = 1 with p even, C29's family at c = 2 with p odd; t = 0 is twins at height c. At c = 1 it is the part of C84 that lowers only even elements. Root claim of I16.
**Evidence.** E22: every t, every c ≤ 2p, p ≤ 10 (committed engine, checked against `Solver`). Ideator (2026-10-30, `scratch/ideator/fresh30b/pbt.rs`, unchecked): every t for p ≤ 9 at c = 1, 2; p ≤ 8 at c = 3; p ≤ 7 at c ∈ {4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13}. The rounds game (C13's high limit) draws at the parity debt for every t, p ≤ 8 (`roundsnear.rs`).
**High base, a candidate strategy** ([E24](../experiments/E24.md)): in the rounds game, Z first picking the top of the highest bumped pair of the current matching (else max) and answering by Hyb2 draws every t in both roles, p ≤ 9. A proof would need a pre-switch class for it (as C23 for AM+T) and debt bounds for the real game; the bounds hold for Z but not for Y (E30: Y's debt reaches 4p − 7 + min R), so the transfer works only from heights about 4p; and no strategy at all keeps one pick per turn below 4p − 8 (E32), so between 2p and 4p the draws of E22 rest on multi-pick turns. A debt bound by the original-pair cost c°(R) (intact pairs' gaps plus the orphans' bottom-up cost) holds at every reached Z start for p ≤ 5, but a third of its members lose under the rule (p = 5: 8112 of 24712; t = 0, ({0, 4, 4, 6}, 0)), so a class for the proof must restrict which subsets occur, not only bound the debt (`scratch/a17/orig_cost.py`). The reached subsets have no simple shape either: per original pair full/one left/gone, the words (equal letters merged) number 4, 10, 24, 46, 76 for p = 2..6 (`scratch/a17`); and the rule itself is wrong off its reached set (({0, 4, 4, 6}, 0): its first pick 6 loses), so the invariant must carry history. A matching μ that Z maintains (start: the original pairs; each round relinks the two orphaned partners; first pick from μ) with d ≤ c(R) holds at every reached Z start (p ≤ 4; μ crosses at 2 of them), but a quarter of its members lose (p = 4: 3280 of 11979, non-crossing μ: 1796 of 7100; ({0, 2, 4, 4}, μ = {0, 4}, {2, 4}, 0)); with d ≤ cost(μ) most lose (`scratch/a17/matching.py`, `matching_c.py`). Shape from [E30](../experiments/E30.md) (p ≤ 6): every reached Z start has d ≤ c(R) and at most one orphan pair; reached pre-switch Y starts at most two. Not sufficient as a class: ({0, 1, 2, 2, 4, 6}, Z at 3) fits it and is a Z loss, so the class must also fix where the orphans may sit. C23's depth class on a virtual block (each intact twin x counted once as x + 1) fails at reached starts from p = 4 (`scratch/a16/vdepth.py`: 31 Z and 12 Y violations among 262 pre-switch starts at p = 5, e.g. ({0, 1, 2, 4, 4, 6}, Z at 3): the orphans 2 and 6 leave a hole at 3 too deep for d + D_Z ≤ |R|/2).
**Band form fails** ([E26](../experiments/E26.md)): unlike blocks (C86), collapsed blocks do not draw on the whole band d ≤ |t|; the failures are diagonal (d = |t|) up to p = 6 and also off the diagonal from p = 7. C85's own entry d = |t| mod 2 holds (E22).
**Tried.** No known strategy proves the high base: always-max, AM+T, twin answer + AM+T, Hyb2 (e.g. offsets {0, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4}, Z trailing by 1: −2) and a token-first opening each lose by ≥ 2 from p = 3 (`amnear.rs`, `amtwin.rs`, `hyb2near.rs`, `hyb2tok.rs`). Composition of pairs played bottom-up (C9) fails on 197 of 1,024 entries.
- Why Hyb2 fails (by hand, 2026-11-04, rounds game on {0, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4}, Z first at debt 1): Z's max pick 4 is a twin; Y answers 1, breaking {0, 1}, and leads the round start ({0, 2, 2, 4}, Y trailing by 2) with c = 4 > 2 (pre-switch). Y takes 4: Z's answer 2 leaves Y first on {0, 2} at debt 0 (Y takes 2, wins by 2); answer 0 leaves Z first on {2, 2} trailing by 2. Z's token opening 1 draws (Y's answers 0, 2, 4 all lead to 0). So the first pick, not the answer, is what breaks; E24 tests bumped-top first picks.
- Paper fact: Hyb2's post-switch guarantee carries over to multisets. With the bottom-up matching of a sorted multiset, removing a matched pair {i, π(i)} gives c(R − {i, π(i)}) = c(R) − |π(i) − i| (twins have gap 0), so at a Y start with e ≥ c(R) the partner answer keeps e' − c' ≥ e − c ≥ 0 and Y keeps the move: Z ≥ draw from every post start. Only pre-switch play and Z's first picks are open.
- c(R) alone is no potential (by hand, 2026-11-04): at d ≥ c the partner reply holds the mover to ≤ −(d − c), but on broken pairs the mover can get less. ({0, 1, 2, 2, 4, 6}, d = 3 = c), a sub-multiset of O_t: taking 6 meets the answer 1, leaving the other side first at debt 2 on {0, 2, 2, 4}, a win by 2 for it; taking 4 or 2 meets the answer 6 and a debt ≥ 5 at c ≤ 3. So the mover gets ≤ −2.
- Twins are not neutral in the rounds game (by hand, 2026-11-04): R({0, 3}, 1) = +2 for the mover (3, then 0), but R({0, 1, 1, 3}, 1) = 0 (the answer 1 to 3 hands the move back at debt 1 on {0, 1}). So no proof can delete twin pairs and reduce to the full block; a simulation "play the twin when the opponent takes one" also breaks at ties, where the round's first picker keeps the move and the simulated and real first pickers differ.
