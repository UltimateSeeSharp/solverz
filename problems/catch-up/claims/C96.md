# C96 Turns as sets

**Statement.** A turn at (R, d) is a set A ⊆ R with a last number f ∈ A such that d ≤ sum(A) and either A = {f} or sum(A ∖ {f}) < d. Every completed turn (picks while behind, ending at the first pick that catches up) is such a set, and conversely every order of A ∖ {f} followed by f is a completed turn; it leaves the opponent at (R ∖ A, sum(A) − d). (a) For d > 0 a nonempty A ⊆ R is a turn iff d ≤ sum(A) < d + max(A) (then f = max A will do); for d = 0 iff A is one number. (b) If R ≠ ∅ and sum(R) ≥ d: V(R, d) ≠ loss iff some turn A has V(R ∖ A, sum(A) − d) ≠ win; V(R, d) ≠ win iff every turn A has V(R ∖ A, sum(A) − d) ≠ loss; V(R, d) = draw iff every turn leaves ≠ loss and some turn leaves a draw. For d > 0 the first two hold with turns given by (a)'s inequalities. (c) After a turn A: sum(R ∖ A) + (sum(A) − d) = sum(R) − d (the new mover's sum plus deficit is the old one's sum minus deficit).
**Use.** Turn-level arguments may pick a subset by arithmetic and play it in any order with its last number last: C72 is stated with such turn sets; C77's blocks summing exactly to d are always turns (d ≤ d < d + max A); C83's and C86's Tried reason with exact-change and two-pick turns; C97 builds on (b). C78 orders the picks within a turn (increasing) and holds the turn-length bound.
