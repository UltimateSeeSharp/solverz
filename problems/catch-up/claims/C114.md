# C114 Two-number turns suffice for the defender

In C1 (every even-total N) and in C110's band, the player holding the draw keeps it in both roles with turns of at most two numbers: each of its turns is one number, or a number below its deficit followed by one that ends the turn, whenever such a turn exists; the opponent's turns are free.

**Use.** I27. A restriction of the defender's strategies that keeps C1: it implies C1 at its roots. One-number turns (the rounds game's) are not enough below the rounds regime; one change number plus one number is.
**Evidence.** [E41](../experiments/E41.md) (committed `ShortTurns`): C110 k = 3..9 at every height ≤ 2(2k + 1), 8,874 positions, and C1 at every even-total N ≤ 20, both roles: no draw lost.
Scratch (`scratch/a19/short/`, strict budget: a first number is allowed only if some second can end the turn):
- `short2.exe ivl 2 20`: {1..N} at every even-total N ≤ 20, both roles: the restricted player holds 0.
- `short.exe 2 3 7 …`: C110 for k = 3..7, every hole, every d ≤ k − 1 of the parity, heights a ≤ 12 (k ≤ 5) and a ≤ 30 (k = 6, 7): no failure in either role. C86's diagonal d = p, p = 3..6, a ≤ 20: no failure (`shortd.exe`; p = 2 fails only at C47's ({2, 3, 4, 5}, 2), a real mover win).
**Tried.** Not true of draws in general: inside {1..14}, 771 + 5,632 of 60,985 draws are lost by the restricted player (moving first, second), e.g. ({1, 2, 4, 5, 6}, 0), ({1, 2, 5, 6}, 4). Narrower turn shapes fail on C1 and C110: one number per turn (k = 3: 45 of 120 positions lost); two numbers only as exact change (C1 already at N = 4); a two-number turn always opening with the smallest number left (C1 at N = 4, 7, 11, …).
**Proof.** Open.
