# C71 Upper-only exact-sum descent

**Statement.** For F(m, k) = [2, m] ∪ [m+3, m+k+2] (the tied residual of Q_N(N−k) after its exact pick, N = m+k+2), m ≥ 6, k ≥ 2: after opening m+4, some block A ⊆ F ∖ {m+4} of sum m+4 is upper-only (every w outside A and above all of A has w ≥ m+3), and for every such w the rest has an upper-only block of sum w.
**Evidence.** Refuted. catch-up-research@b0853b4, `CatchUp/BoCowgill/Earlier/UniformExactSumDescentObstruction.lean` (CU-L038), `no_two_upper_only_steps`: the first block must be {4, m} (`first_block_forced`), and the next permitted witness m+3 has no upper-only block (`no_second_upper_only_block`). A statement about a certificate class, not a game value (it does not say m+3 loses), so nothing to recheck with `position_value`. CU-L039 (informal, not Lean): for q ≥ 3, at most ⌊(q−1)/2⌋ disjoint sets of integers ≥ 2 sum to q, so their consecutive-lower-maximum forcing scheme on F(m, k), assuming no lower witness and no upper counter in an exact block, fails for m ≥ 2k+2; a known lower-intruder repair (consuming {2,4,5}) leaves the family. An exact-sum step (delete a block of sum d, open w) lowers the effective total d + sum(R) by exactly 2d (`effective_total_add_twice_deficit_after_exact_block_opening`, `CatchUp/BoCowgill/EffectiveTotalDescent.lean`, verified), so the descent terminates, but it does not keep a bounded-hole family (CU-L020, historical).
Rules out: a descent in the tail length k by C77's exact-sum steps with upper witnesses only; C73's fourth and fifth endpoints hold for fixed k.
