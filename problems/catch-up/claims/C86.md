# C86 The draw band at every height (BAND)

For all p ≥ 1, a ≥ 1 and 0 ≤ d ≤ p with d ≡ p (mod 2), except (p, a, d) = (2, 2, 2): V({a..a+2p−1}, d) = draw.

**Use.** Root claim of I17. Implies C12 (d = p mod 2) and, through C27's families (Lean: `CatchUp.conjecture_of_band`, `CatchUp/Band.lean`, verified 2026-11-04) (Lean `conjecture_of_two_families`: {1..N} at 0 for N ≡ 0 mod 4, {2..N} at 1 for N ≡ 3 mod 4), C1. Lean has it for a ≥ 2p − 1 (`block_row_draw_hyb2`, C24). Relation to C37: at a = 1, {1..2p} ⊎ {d} is complete (γ = 1), so LG gives m ≥ −1, and with the parity (m ≡ d + p ≡ 0) the non-loss half for every d ≤ 2p; C86 adds the half "the mover does not win" for d ≤ p (above p it can win, C86's run 4).
**Link to C84.** At a = 1, ({1..2p}, d) is the position after opening the duplicate d of the tied multiset {1..2p} ⊎ {d}, which is C84's member with L = 2p + 1 and ε_j = 1 exactly for j > d (parity deficit 0). So C86 at a = 1 makes that opening a draw, and the level law (C28) gives C84 for these members. Likewise at every height with 1 ≤ d < a: ({a..a+2p−1}, d) is the opening d of the tied set {d} ∪ {a..a+2p−1}, so C86 and C28 (Lean `level_law`, sets) give that these odd-size sets draw at a tie, e.g. {1, 3, 4} (opening 1 leaves ({3, 4}, 1), a draw by C7).
**Evidence.** [E27](../experiments/E27.md) (committed engine) and the ideator's `rows.rs` (own solver): every p ≤ 10, a ≤ 2p + 1 (505 entries); the only non-draw in the band is {2, 3, 4, 5} at deficit 2 (C47). Lean: p ≤ 4 verified (`band_draw_le_four`). Outside the band at p = 2 (C93, verified): for a ≥ 5, {a..a+3} loses at every other d ≤ a (e.g. d = p + 2) and is a mover win at a < d ≤ a + 3 (`value_top_four`). Between integer heights the band is not invariant (C88 refuted: interior entries flip on intervals such as (1, 3/2)), so the statement holds at integer heights only, and a rounds-structure strategy reaches down to a ≈ 3p/2 only (E28), though no simple Hyb2 variant gets below 2p − 1 (E31). At real heights the lowest entry d = p mod 2 is a draw for every h ≥ 1/2 (C32, p ≤ 8). The rest of the row is not height-invariant (run 4: 280 of 679 entries differ at low heights, all in the trailer's favour).
**Tried.** By hand, how the diagonal survives at a = 1 (2026-11-04): ({1..6}, 3) draws because the trailer's two-pick turn {1, 2} makes exact change to a tie on {3, 4, 5, 6} (C11), and after the opening 6 the reply 3 is exact change to a tie on {1, 2, 4, 5} (C11). This does not generalize (`scratch/a16/exact_pairs.py`, blocks {1..2p}, p ≤ 5, every defender turn reached under drawing play): a turn of whole matched pairs making exact change exists at about 10% of them and loses more often than it draws (p = 5: 822 fail, 483 draw; e.g. ({1, 2, 4}, 3): {1, 2} loses). So the low-height band has no simple turn shape either. A potential per height in C25's terms cannot work as is: the least capped height H(R, d, mover) of the one-pick rounds game is not a function of (|R|, max R − min R, o₂ − min R, d) on subsets of {0..7} (1001 clashes over 522 feature values), and on blocks it is not monotone in d (Z on {0..5}: 5, 4, 3 at d = 0, 1, 3) (`scratch/a17/height.py`). No fixed one-number-at-a-time Z strategy of the obvious kinds draws the band in the real game either (always max, exact change else max, smallest catching-up number, exact change else smallest: 148–169 failing entries for p ≤ 5, from p = 1 or 2 on; `scratch/a18/simple_z.py`). Pairing strategies (answer Y's last number by its adjacent or mirror partner, else max or min) fail worse: 130–168 of 172 entries for p ≤ 5 (`scratch/a18/pairing_z.py`). The band does not extend to unions of p separated adjacent pairs {x, x + 1} inside {1..14}: non-draws at d = p (13, 39, 24 for p = 2, 3, 4, both signs, e.g. {5, 6, 8, 9} at 2 loses) and at d = 2 < p = 4 next to C47's {2, 3, 4, 5} ({2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12} at 2, a mover win); below d = p and away from {2..5} all draw (`scratch/a18/pair_unions.py`). So contiguity matters at d = p. One height below C24 (a = 2p − 2), by hand at p = 4 on C24's tight line (Z 7, Y 2; Y 3, Z 4; Y 5, Z 6): Hyb2's smallest valid answers raise Y's debt 5 → 6 → 7 and leave {0, 1}, which Y takes in one turn at a = 6. At ({0, 1, 5, 6}, Y at 6) Y's pick 5 leaves Z only the answer 6 (0 or 1 lose outright), so a repair must come earlier. Not at Y's 3: answering 6 instead of 4 leaves Y trailing by 8 > a + min R = 6 on {0, 1, 4, 5}, and Y takes 6 and 7 in one turn, leads by 5 and wins (first written here as a repair, wrong); the cap allows only j ≤ 4 there, Hyb2's own answer. So on this line the repair has to come at Z's first pick. Indeed under the cap Hyb2's first pick 7 loses for Z: after Y's answer 2, Y moves at debt 5 on {0, 1, 3, 4, 5, 6} and wins by taking 3 (Z's answers allowed by the cap are 0, 1, 4; 4 is the line above, 0 and 1 lose to Y's next pick 6). The real entry (4, 6, 0) is a draw, so at 2p − 2 Z needs another first pick or play that uses multi-pick turns; E28 separates the two. At p = 3, a = 4 = 2p − 2, d = 1 (by hand, capped rounds game): Z first, Hyb2 survives every line checked; Y first, Hyb2 fails (Y 3, Z 5; at ({0, 1, 2, 4}, Y at 3) Y takes 2 and every answer loses: 4 lifts Y's debt to 5 > 4 + 0 and Y takes 8 and 9 in one turn, 0 and 1 lose outright), while the tie answer 2 to Y's 3 (Y's debt exactly 0) survives each of Y's replies 5, 4, 1, 0. So a repaired Hyb2 at 2p − 2 answers with the tie-making j = i − e where Hyb2's answer would later lift a debt above the cap.
