# Experiment finds the object
Amateurs win mostly at `find`: an object (tile, graph) that a computer or a referee can check, found by play, persistence and software (S10–S12). Simulation and numerics find what a proof then confirms (S13, S14); a blind benchmark with a checkable score lets an outsider method prove itself (S15).
Stuck on a `find` or `bound` problem: build, play and simulate, then hand the object to a checker.

### S10 Einstein tile
Stuck: to 2023 (partial answers 1988, 2010) · `find` · used: T1366, T839
- Unlock: the "hat" polykite, found by playing in tiling software and with cardboard; aperiodicity via metatiles and substitution, a computer-assisted combinatorial proof, and a geometric incommensurability argument for a continuum of shapes. The chiral "spectre" followed.
- From: an amateur's play: David Smith, retired print technician.
- Source: [Wikipedia: Einstein problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_problem), [arXiv:2303.10798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10798), [Wikipedia: David Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Smith_(amateur_mathematician))

### S11 Convex pentagon tilings
Stuck: 1975–1977 · `find` · used: T1366
- Unlock: a reader of Martin Gardner's column refuted the claimed completeness of the list and found four new pentagon types, with her own notation; the claim was false (S4).
- From: an amateur's play: Marjorie Rice, homemaker with a high-school education.
- Source: [Wikipedia: Marjorie Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Rice)

### S12 Hadwiger–Nelson lower bound
Stuck: 1950–2018 · `bound` `find` · used: T123, T1355
- Unlock: copies of the Moser spindle combined into a 20,425-vertex unit-distance graph, shrunk to 1,581, its non-4-colourability verified by computer (chromatic number ≥ 5). In Polymath16, Mixon–Alexeev cut it to 1,577 and Heule to 874, then 826, within days; Heule's proof minimization reached 553 by 30 May 2018, and Parts' 509 (2020) is the smallest known.
- From: an amateur: Aubrey de Grey, a biologist who learnt graph theory through Othello friends.
- Source: [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/decades-old-graph-problem-yields-to-amateur-mathematician-20180417/), [MathWorld: Hadwiger–Nelson problem](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Hadwiger-NelsonProblem.html), [arXiv:1805.12181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12181), [arXiv:2010.12665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12665)

### S13 Kelvin problem
Stuck: 1887–1993 · `find` · used: T155
- Unlock: Weaire and Phelan found, by computer simulation of foams, a structure with 0.3% less surface area than Kelvin's, which no one had beaten for over 100 years.
- From: physical simulation, as a `find` engine for a continuous extremal problem.
- Source: [Wikipedia: Weaire–Phelan structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaire%E2%80%93Phelan_structure)

### S14 Figure-eight three-body orbit
Stuck: 1993–2000 · `find` · used: T1310, T676, T843
- Unlock: Moore found the orbit numerically; Chenciner–Montgomery proved it exists by minimizing the action over a symmetry class.
- From: numerics, then the calculus of variations.
- Source: [Wikipedia: Three-body problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-body_problem), [arXiv:math/0011268](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0011268)

### S15 Protein structure prediction (AlphaFold)
Stuck: 1994–2020 · `find` · used: T1357
- Unlock: a learned model judged by CASP, a double-blind community benchmark; AlphaFold 2 scored above 90 GDT on about two thirds of CASP14 targets.
- From: machine learning, proved on a blind benchmark.
- Source: [Wikipedia: AlphaFold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaFold), [Wikipedia: CASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASP)
