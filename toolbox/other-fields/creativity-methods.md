# Creativity methods
Prompts and procedures for new ideas: provocation, random links, cross-field analogy, checklists, separated modes of thinking and play.

### T1359 Lateral thinking
`find` `prove` `bound` `compute` · stage: practice
aka: de Bono, challenge, provocation, state the impossible, disprove the taken-for-granted, fractionation, why is it so
- What: challenge ("why is it so?"), provocation (state the impossible and move from it), disproving the taken-for-granted, fractionation.
- Fits when: hypotheses are taken for granted; ask why each is there, provoke ("the extremal object is random", "the bound is polynomial") and extract a principle.
- Lean: —
- Example: [Wikipedia: Lateral thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking).
- See also: T1360, T1362

### T1360 Random stimulus
`find` `prove` `bound` `compute` · stage: practice
aka: random word, random object, random theorem, forced bridge, unrelated area, de Bono
- What: connects the problem to a random word or object.
- Fits when: ideas have dried up; force a random theorem or technique from an unrelated area onto the problem and look for a bridge.
- Lean: —
- Example: de Bono's "nose" gave a photocopier that smells when out of paper ([Wikipedia: Lateral thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking)).
- See also: T1359, T1363

### T1361 Forced connections (synectics)
`find` `prove` `bound` `compute` · stage: practice
aka: synectics, make the strange familiar, make the familiar strange, recast as a physical system, code, game or network, Spielman, electrical networks, Kadison–Singer
- What: makes the strange familiar and the familiar strange by analogy.
- Fits when: describe the object as a physical system, a code, a game or a network and use that field's intuitions.
- Lean: —
- Example: [Wikipedia: Synectics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synectics); Spielman found Weaver's conjecture nearly identical to a question about electrical networks ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/computer-scientists-solve-kadison-singer-problem-20151124/)).
- See also: T1363

### T1362 SCAMPER
`find` `prove` `bound` `compute` · stage: practice
aka: Substitute, Combine, Adjust, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Reverse, checklist on the statement, drop a hypothesis, reverse the inequality, change signs, Huang
- What: a checklist: Substitute, Combine, Adjust, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Reverse.
- Fits when: varying the statement: swap the field, combine two conjectures, magnify a parameter, drop a hypothesis, reverse the inequality, change signs.
- Lean: —
- Example: [Wikipedia: SCAMPER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCAMPER); Huang's fix was to flip signs of matrix entries ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/mathematician-solves-computer-science-conjecture-in-two-pages-20190725/)).
- See also: T1313, T1372

### T1363 Bisociation
`find` `prove` `bound` `compute` · stage: practice
aka: Koestler, fusion of two frames, pair two fields, combinatorics with information theory, number theory with dynamics, Furstenberg, ergodic proof of Szemerédi
- What: creation as the fusion of two unrelated frames.
- Fits when: pair the problem's field with a second one on purpose (combinatorics with information theory, number theory with dynamics).

- Example: [Wikipedia: Bisociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisociation); Furstenberg's ergodic proof of Szemerédi ([Wikipedia: Szemerédi's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szemer%C3%A9di%27s_theorem)).

### T1366 Hands-on play
`find` · stage: explore
aka: physical models, interactive viewers, cardboard cut-outs, PolyForm Puzzle Solver, tiles, einstein tile, the hat, David Smith, Marjorie Rice, pentagon tilings, play before theory
- What: explores with physical or software models before theory.
- Fits when: a `find` problem with a visual object (tiles, graphs, colourings); build cheap interactive viewers and play.
- Lean: —
- Example: Smith experimented in PolyForm Puzzle Solver and checked the hat with cardboard cut-outs ([Wikipedia: David Smith (amateur mathematician)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Smith_(amateur_mathematician))); Rice invented her own notation at her kitchen table ([Wikipedia: Marjorie Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Rice)).
- See also: T1333, T1358
