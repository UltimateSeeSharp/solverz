# Scepticism

How careful mathematicians doubt a result; signs to look closer, never
reasons to reject on their own.

- It seems too easy: find where the difficulty went.
- It proves too much: the same argument would prove something false.
- A sudden simplification after a long hard stretch: breakthroughs look like
  this too; check exactly that step.
- Drift: the argument stops mentioning what the problem is about.
- A twist on a popular method that experts supposedly missed.
- Reformulating until a restatement is easier because it is wrong.
- A contradiction reached by loose computation.
- A missed case; listing every case shows it.
- Pieces that are each harder than the whole.
- Asking what the key new idea is, and why earlier attempts fell short.
- Asking why it avoids the known obstructions, and what its milestones are.
- Asking whether one sign error would destroy it.
- AI-written work: fluent is not evidence, errors look flawless, a claimed
  source counts only once opened, and Lean certifies the statement, not the
  informal proof.
